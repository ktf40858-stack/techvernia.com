What is Genspark?

Genspark is an AI search engine that replaces the traditional list of blue links with dynamically generated "Sparkpages" — comprehensive, structured knowledge summaries created specifically for each search query. Founded by Eric Jing and Kay Zhu (both former Baidu executives) and launched in 2024, Genspark raised $60M+ in funding and quickly built a reputation for producing more complete, less ad-cluttered search results than Google for informational queries.

The core innovation behind Sparkpages is the multi-agent approach: rather than one AI model synthesizing sources, Genspark deploys multiple specialized AI agents in parallel — a research agent finding sources, a fact-checking agent verifying claims, a structuring agent organizing the content, and a writing agent producing the final page. The result is a more thorough and better-organized summary than single-model approaches.

In 2026, Genspark has expanded with Autopilot mode (a research agent similar to Perplexity Deep Research), real-time data integration (stock prices, weather, flight data), and a personalization layer that adapts Sparkpages to your expertise level and preferred depth of coverage.

Key Features

Sparkpages — Dynamic Knowledge Summaries

Every Genspark search produces a Sparkpage: a comprehensive, structured document covering the query from multiple angles. Unlike a bulleted AI answer, Sparkpages have multiple sections, comparisons, timelines, and contextual depth depending on the query type. Search for "climate change solutions" and you get sections on mitigation strategies, current policy approaches, technology options, and economic considerations — each with sourced facts. The structure adapts to the topic rather than using a fixed template.

Multi-Agent Research Architecture

Genspark's multi-agent system runs multiple research processes in parallel, which produces more comprehensive coverage than sequential single-model search. One agent focuses on finding diverse sources, another on extracting key facts, another on identifying conflicting information and resolving it, and another on organizing the output logically. Users can see which agents contributed to which sections of the Sparkpage, providing unusual transparency into the AI research process.

Autopilot Deep Research Mode

For complex research tasks, Genspark's Autopilot mode runs an extended multi-step research loop similar to Perplexity Deep Research — but typically faster (1–3 minutes) at the cost of slightly less depth. Autopilot is well-suited for getting a solid first draft of research on a topic before going deeper with specialized tools. The output Sparkpage from Autopilot includes a methodology section showing which sources were consulted and how many research steps were taken.

Zero Advertising Model

Genspark is explicitly built without advertising — no sponsored results, no ad-driven ranking, no affiliate links disguised as organic content. This matters for information quality: search results that depend on ad revenue have structural incentives to surface content from well-funded advertisers over the most informative sources. Genspark's subscription revenue model aligns its incentives with user information quality, not advertiser spend.

Final Verdict

Genspark earns its place as one of the best AI search alternatives to Perplexity through its unique Sparkpages format and multi-agent research architecture. For informational queries where you need comprehensive, well-organized knowledge — not just a quick answer — Sparkpages consistently produce more complete and better-structured output than Perplexity's standard search or Google's AI Overviews.

Where Genspark falls slightly short of Perplexity is in deep research citation quality and export functionality for the Pro use case. But at $9.99/month vs. Perplexity's $20/month, it offers excellent value for users who primarily use AI search for daily informational research rather than producing formal research reports. The zero-ad model and Autopilot mode make Genspark a daily driver worth recommending alongside or instead of Google for knowledge workers.

Best for: Knowledge workers, students, and researchers who want fast, comprehensive, ad-free answers to complex informational queries — and a cheaper alternative to Perplexity Pro for daily research needs.
