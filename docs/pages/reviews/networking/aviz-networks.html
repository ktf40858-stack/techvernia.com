Overview

Aviz Networks is an AI-powered open networking management platform built around SONiC (Software for Open Networking in the Cloud), the open-source network operating system originally developed by Microsoft. Founded in 2021, Aviz helps enterprises and service providers deploy, manage, and troubleshoot SONiC-based open networking infrastructure at scale — replacing proprietary networking stacks with open, AI-managed alternatives.

Open networking represents a major shift in data center infrastructure: instead of buying proprietary switches from Cisco or Arista with locked-in software, organizations use commodity hardware running SONiC with AI management tools like Aviz to achieve equivalent functionality at dramatically lower cost. Aviz provides the AI intelligence layer that makes SONiC manageable at enterprise scale — something difficult to achieve with open-source tools alone.

In 2026, Aviz has positioned itself as the leading commercial SONiC management platform as hyperscalers and large enterprises accelerate open networking adoption. The platform includes AI-powered network health monitoring, automated troubleshooting, configuration management, and analytics for complex multi-vendor SONiC environments.

Key Features

SONiC Network Management

Centralized management for SONiC-based switches from multiple hardware vendors (Edgecore, Dell, Celestica, etc.). Unified interface replacing vendor-specific tools.

AI Network Health Monitoring

Continuous AI monitoring of network health metrics. Detects anomalies, predicts issues before they cause outages, and provides proactive alerts.

Automated Troubleshooting

AI diagnoses network issues by correlating metrics across devices and suggesting remediation steps. Reduces MTTR (Mean Time to Repair) significantly.

Configuration Management

Version-controlled configuration management for SONiC devices. Push, rollback, and audit configurations across the entire network fleet.

Analytics & Visibility

Deep traffic analytics, capacity planning, and network telemetry. Understand traffic patterns and plan infrastructure investments with data.

Multi-Vendor Support

Manages SONiC deployments across different hardware vendors in a single pane of glass. No vendor lock-in for hardware or software.

How It Compares

Aviz Networks vs Cisco DNA Center

Cisco DNA Center manages Cisco's proprietary networking stack. Aviz manages open SONiC networking. They serve fundamentally different infrastructure philosophies — Cisco DNA Center is the right choice for Cisco-committed organizations, while Aviz is purpose-built for open networking adopters.

Aviz Networks vs Apstra (Juniper)

Apstra is an intent-based networking platform for data center automation. Aviz focuses specifically on SONiC management and open networking. Different but competitive in automation goals — Apstra spans more proprietary platforms while Aviz goes deeper on the SONiC/open networking stack.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SONiC and why is it significant?+
SONiC (Software for Open Networking in the Cloud) is an open-source network operating system originally developed by Microsoft for its Azure data centers. It runs on commodity networking hardware from multiple vendors, allowing organizations to separate hardware from software and avoid vendor lock-in with traditional proprietary networking stacks like Cisco IOS.
How does Aviz compare to just using SONiC directly?+
SONiC is powerful but complex to manage at scale without additional tooling. Aviz provides the AI management layer — monitoring, troubleshooting, configuration management, and analytics — that makes SONiC operationally viable for enterprise teams without deep SONiC engineering expertise on staff.
What hardware vendors does Aviz support?+
Aviz supports SONiC-capable switches from multiple hardware vendors including Edgecore Networks, Dell EMC, Celestica, Accton, and others. This multi-vendor support is key to avoiding hardware lock-in alongside software lock-in avoidance.
Is Aviz suitable for smaller networks?+
Aviz has a community edition for smaller deployments, but its full value is realized at enterprise scale where AI monitoring, configuration management, and analytics deliver the most ROI. Small networks may find simpler management tools sufficient for their needs.
