Overview

CoCounsel is Thomson Reuters' AI assistant for legal, tax, audit and accounting professionals. CoCounsel Legal combines research, analysis and drafting, grounded in Westlaw primary law, Practical Law guidance and your own documents.

It is sold to law firms of every size, corporate legal departments and government offices such as courts, prosecutors and public defenders. Thomson Reuters says CoCounsel has 1 million users.

Its main differentiator is content: answers link back to Westlaw and Practical Law. You describe a task in plain language, and CoCounsel builds a step-by-step plan you can inspect before it runs multistep work.

Key Features

Research on Westlaw Content

Research and analysis draw on Westlaw primary law and Practical Law guidance, with citations.

Tabular Analysis

Review up to 10,000 documents against 100 questions, with every answer traceable to its source.

Drafting with Linked Citations

Turns a single request into a draft whose citations link back for verification.

Westlaw Brief Builder

Builds litigation briefs from issue framing to argument structure, with the attorney reviewing each step.

Workspaces

Keeps documents, templates, conversations and matter history together.

Integrations

Connects to iManage, NetDocuments, SharePoint, Box, Litify, Smokeball, Microsoft Word, Outlook and Teams.

Pros & Cons

Advantages

  • Grounded in Westlaw and Practical Law content
  • Shows its step-by-step plan before running a task
  • Handles large document reviews (up to 10,000 documents)
  • Wide range of document management integrations
  • Listed as SOC 2 and ISO 42001 certified
  • Customer data is not used to train the underlying models

Disadvantages

  • Prices for firms with more than 10 attorneys require a quote
  • The full CoCounsel Legal plan is bundled with Westlaw Advantage
  • A free trial is only offered for CoCounsel Essentials
  • Best value depends on already using Thomson Reuters content

Best Use Cases

CoCounsel Excels For:

  • Firms that already rely on Westlaw and Practical Law
  • Litigators drafting and checking briefs
  • Large document reviews against a fixed set of questions
  • Government legal offices, courts and public defenders

May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Teams that do not want a Thomson Reuters subscription
  • Buyers who need published enterprise pricing
  • Tax-only research teams (a specialist such as Blue J may fit better)

Comparison

CoCounsel vs Competitors

  • vs Harvey: Harvey sells its own agents, Vault and Knowledge products to large firms and in-house teams by demo. CoCounsel's edge is direct grounding in Westlaw and Practical Law.
  • vs Everlaw: Everlaw is an e-discovery platform for reviewing evidence at scale. CoCounsel is a research and drafting assistant; Everlaw lists Thomson Reuters among the AI tools it connects to.
  • vs Blue J: Blue J is tax-only and publishes a $1,498 per user per year price. CoCounsel covers broad legal work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is CoCounsel?

Thomson Reuters' AI assistant for legal research, document review and drafting, grounded in Westlaw and Practical Law.

How is CoCounsel related to Casetext?

CoCounsel was first offered by Casetext, which Thomson Reuters acquired in 2023. Thomson Reuters now sells it.

How much does CoCounsel cost?

Prices for firms with 1 to 10 attorneys are shown on the Thomson Reuters site. Larger firms need a quote.

Is there a free trial?

A free trial is offered for CoCounsel Essentials. The other plans are sold through a free demo.

Does Thomson Reuters train models on my data?

Thomson Reuters says user content is not used to train CoCounsel or third-party models, and calls to AI model providers use zero retention.

What does CoCounsel integrate with?

iManage, NetDocuments, SharePoint, HighQ, Box, Litify, Smokeball, and Microsoft Word, Outlook and Teams.

Final Verdict

4.7/5
Strong Choice for Westlaw Users

CoCounsel is the natural AI assistant for firms already on Westlaw and Practical Law, with traceable research and large-scale document review. Firms outside the Thomson Reuters ecosystem should compare it with independent tools first.
