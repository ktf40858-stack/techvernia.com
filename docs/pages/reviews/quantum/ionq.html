Overview

IonQ builds trapped-ion quantum computers. Its current systems are Aria, Forte, Forte Enterprise and Tempo. Forte has 36 qubits, with published error rates of 0.02% for one-qubit gates and 0.4% for two-qubit gates; Forte Enterprise is a 36-qubit, rack-mountable system for on-premises use.

Access goes through IonQ Quantum Cloud, which offers a Cloud Console, noisy and ideal simulators, on-demand QPU access and reserved time, and through partner clouds Amazon Braket, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud. IonQ supports Qiskit, Cirq, CUDA-Q, tket, Q#, PennyLane, ProjectQ and other SDKs.

Simulator access is free. Direct QPU access uses volume-based or custom pricing, while partner clouds publish their own rates. Academic researchers can apply for up to $10,000 in research credits.

Key Features

IonQ Quantum Cloud

Cloud Console to submit and track jobs, with organization management for enterprise teams.

Simulators

Ideal and noisy simulators for every stage of algorithm development, free to start.

Forte systems

36-qubit trapped-ion systems; Forte lists 0.02% one-qubit and 0.4% two-qubit gate error.

Multi-cloud access

IonQ hardware is also available through Amazon Braket, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud.

Broad SDK support

Qiskit, Cirq, CUDA-Q, tket, Q#, PennyLane, ProjectQ and more; IonQ's compiler maps standard gates to native ones.

Reserved and on-prem options

Reserved QPU time for scheduled workloads, and Forte Enterprise for on-premises deployment.

Pros & Cons

Advantages

  • Available on all three major clouds as well as IonQ's own
  • Free simulator access to start
  • Works with most popular quantum SDKs
  • Published gate error rates for Forte
  • Research credits of up to $10,000 for academics
  • On-premises option with Forte Enterprise

Disadvantages

  • Direct QPU pricing is not published (volume-based or custom)
  • Expensive per run on partner clouds: $0.08 per shot on Braket, $168.195 minimum per program on Azure Forte with error mitigation
  • On Braket, error mitigation requires at least 2,500 shots per task
  • The free tier covers simulators, not QPU time
  • 36 qubits on Forte, fewer than some superconducting systems with 100+ qubits

Pricing

IonQ pricing depends on how you access its systems:

IonQ Quantum Cloud

Free Access tier for simulators. QPU access uses volume-based pricing, with custom pricing on request: contact sales.

Amazon Braket

IonQ Forte: $0.30 per task plus $0.08 per shot. Braket Direct reservation: $7,000 per hour.

Azure Quantum

Per gate-shot pricing; minimum per program $97.50 on Aria 1 and $168.195 on Forte with error mitigation. Aria-Forte subscription: $25,000/month.

Research Credits

Best Use Cases

IonQ Excels For:

  • Algorithms that benefit from full qubit connectivity
  • Teams that want the same hardware across AWS, Azure and Google Cloud

  • Organizations evaluating an on-premises quantum system

May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Budget-limited projects that need many shots

  • Experiments that need more than 36 qubits

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I access IonQ quantum computers?

Through IonQ Quantum Cloud, or through Amazon Braket, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud.

Is there free access?

Yes, for simulators: the Free Access tier lets you sign up and explore with IonQ's simulators. QPU access is a separate tier.

How much does IonQ cost?

Direct QPU access is volume-based or custom-priced. On Amazon Braket, Forte costs $0.30 per task plus $0.08 per shot; on Azure, pricing is per gate-shot with per-program minimums.

How many qubits do IonQ systems have?

Forte and Forte Enterprise have 36 qubits. IonQ also lists Aria and Tempo.

Which SDKs work with IonQ?

Qiskit, Cirq, CUDA-Q, tket, Q#, PennyLane, ProjectQ, TensorFlow and others, plus Braket compatibility.

Does IonQ offer research credits?

Yes. Academic researchers may qualify for up to $10,000 through the Research Credits Program.
