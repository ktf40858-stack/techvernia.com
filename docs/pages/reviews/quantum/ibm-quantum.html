Overview

IBM Quantum gives cloud access to IBM's fleet of 100+ qubit quantum computers through IBM Quantum Platform, which also hosts documentation and courses. Current processors include Heron and Nighthawk; IBM says early demonstrations on Nighthawk r2 run up to 12x faster than on Heron.

Programs are written with Qiskit, IBM's open-source quantum software stack (v2.6.0), which supports Python, C and C++. Qiskit includes a transpiler, primitives for running experiments, modular addons and a catalog of Qiskit Functions, and it also has plugins for other hardware such as IonQ and Amazon Braket.

The free Open Plan includes 10 minutes of runtime per month. Paid plans are billed per minute, and organizations can join the IBM Quantum Network or install an on-premises system.

Key Features

IBM Quantum Platform

Web platform to access IBM quantum computers and manage usage, with administration, analytics and learning tools.

Heron and Nighthawk processors

A fleet of 100+ qubit processors; IBM cites circuits of up to five thousand gates.

Qiskit SDK

Open-source stack to build circuits, compile them with the transpiler, run them with primitives and visualize results.

Qiskit addons

Modular building blocks for mapping, optimizing, executing and post-processing workloads (for example circuit cutting and SQD).

Qiskit Functions

Catalog of IBM and third-party services for algorithm and application development.

IBM Quantum Network

Community of academic, industry and startup partners; membership requires a minimum purchase.

Pros & Cons

Advantages

  • Free access to real 100+ qubit hardware (10 minutes per month)
  • Qiskit is widely used: IBM reports 13M downloads and 7K+ dependent projects
  • Per-minute prices are published for every self-serve plan
  • Documentation and courses hosted on the platform
  • Clear path to larger commitments: Flex, Premium, Network membership or on-prem

Disadvantages

  • Paid usage is expensive: pay-as-you-go starts at $96 per minute
  • Flex and Premium need annual minimums (400 and 5,200 minutes) and a contract
  • Only IBM hardware is available on the platform
  • The free plan is limited to 10 minutes of runtime per month
  • On-premises systems are quote-only

Best Use Cases

IBM Quantum Excels For:

  • Students and developers learning on real quantum hardware for free
  • Researchers running large circuits on 100+ qubit processors
  • Teams standardizing on Qiskit
  • Enterprises that want a partnership through the IBM Quantum Network

May Not Be Ideal For:

  • Projects that need several hardware vendors in one place
  • Heavy users on a tight budget, given per-minute rates
  • Annealing-based optimization workloads

Frequently Asked Questions

Is IBM Quantum free?

Yes, with limits. The Open Plan gives up to 10 minutes of runtime per month on 100+ qubit quantum computers.

How much do paid plans cost?

Pay-As-You-Go starts at $96 per minute, Flex at $72 per minute (400 minutes per year minimum) and Premium at $48 per minute (5,200 minutes per year minimum). On-prem systems are quoted.

Which processors are available?

IBM lists Heron and Nighthawk processors with 100+ qubits.

Is Qiskit free?

Yes. Qiskit is open source and supports Python, C and C++.

Can I use Qiskit with other hardware?

Yes. Qiskit has hardware plugins for providers including IonQ and Amazon Braket.

What is the IBM Quantum Network?

A community of academic, industry and startup partners working with IBM. Membership requires a minimum purchase.
