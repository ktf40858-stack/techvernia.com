Overview

QpiAI is an Indian quantum computing and AI company founded in Bangalore in 2019, making it one of India's earliest and most prominent quantum technology startups. QpiAI focuses on building quantum hardware, quantum software, and hybrid quantum-classical AI algorithms — positioning India as a participant in the global quantum computing race.

QpiAI's approach combines quantum hardware development (superconducting qubits), a quantum software stack (QpiAI Pro platform), and practical applications in drug discovery, materials simulation, optimization, and AI/ML acceleration. The company has received funding from the Indian government's National Quantum Mission and private investors.

In 2026, QpiAI has expanded its cloud quantum computing access platform and established partnerships with Indian pharmaceutical companies, research institutions (IITs, IISc), and government agencies. The company represents the Indian quantum ecosystem's emergence as a serious player alongside US, European, and Chinese quantum efforts.

Key Features

Quantum Hardware

Superconducting qubit quantum processors developed indigenously in India. Active development toward higher qubit counts and improved coherence times.

QpiAI Pro Platform

Cloud-based quantum computing platform with a Python SDK. Access quantum hardware, simulators, and hybrid algorithms from a unified interface.

Hybrid Quantum-Classical AI

Quantum-enhanced machine learning algorithms that combine quantum circuits with classical neural networks. Applications in optimization and simulation.

Drug Discovery Applications

Quantum simulation of molecular interactions for pharmaceutical R&D. Quantum advantage applications for molecular docking and protein folding.

Quantum-Safe Cryptography

Post-quantum cryptography tools for securing communications against future quantum attacks. Relevant for enterprise security planning.

Education & Training

Quantum computing courses and certification programs for Indian institutions and enterprises. Building India's quantum talent pipeline.

How It Compares

QpiAI vs IBM Quantum

IBM offers the most mature quantum computing platform globally. QpiAI is India's domestic alternative with national strategic importance and growing capabilities.

QpiAI vs D-Wave

D-Wave specializes in quantum annealing for optimization. QpiAI pursues gate-based quantum computing with hybrid AI applications. Different hardware approaches.

Final Verdict

Our Recommendation

QpiAI holds a unique and important position as India's leading full-stack quantum computing company. In the context of India's National Quantum Mission and the strategic importance of quantum technology sovereignty, QpiAI's work building domestic quantum hardware and software is nationally significant. For Indian enterprises and research institutions exploring quantum computing, QpiAI offers the advantage of domestic partnership, government backing, and a growing platform. As India's quantum ecosystem matures, QpiAI is positioned at its center.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is QpiAI significant for India?+
QpiAI is building India's domestic quantum computing capability — hardware, software, and applications — rather than depending entirely on US or European quantum platforms. With India's National Quantum Mission investing ₹6,000 crore in quantum technology, QpiAI is a key beneficiary and executor of India's quantum strategy.
Can international researchers use QpiAI's platform?+
QpiAI's cloud platform is accessible internationally via their website. However, the primary focus is on Indian institutions and partnerships. International users may find IBM Quantum or Amazon Braket more feature-complete for immediate needs.
What quantum hardware does QpiAI use?+
QpiAI develops superconducting qubit-based quantum processors, the same fundamental technology used by IBM and Google. The company is progressing toward higher qubit counts and improved error rates as part of its hardware roadmap.
Is QpiAI involved in quantum-safe cryptography?+
Yes — QpiAI has a post-quantum cryptography offering, helping organizations prepare for the security threat that large-scale quantum computers would pose to current encryption (RSA, ECC). This is increasingly relevant as quantum hardware progresses.
