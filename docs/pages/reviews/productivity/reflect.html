What is Reflect?

Reflect is a private, AI-powered note-taking application built on two core principles that are rare in the software industry: genuine end-to-end encryption and an AI assistant that processes your notes locally — meaning your thoughts never leave your device in plaintext, and no third-party server ever reads your personal knowledge base.

Founded in 2021, Reflect draws inspiration from the networked note-taking philosophy popularized by Roam Research and Obsidian. Notes are not organized in folders; they exist as a connected graph, linked together through bi-directional references. Every time you mention one note inside another, a backlink is automatically created, allowing you to traverse your knowledge non-linearly — exactly the way human memory actually works.

What makes Reflect stand apart from its competitors is the combination of this networked structure with privacy-preserving AI. The built-in AI assistant — powered by GPT-4 — can summarize, elaborate, or brainstorm within your existing notes, but it does so using an architecture that prevents your raw note content from being stored or analyzed on Reflect's servers. For privacy-conscious professionals, researchers, lawyers, and knowledge workers who handle sensitive material, this is a genuinely meaningful distinction.

Reflect also includes a Daily Notes system — a fresh, dated page created automatically each day — as well as tight integration with Readwise (for importing highlights from books and articles), native calendar sync (so meetings appear in your daily note automatically), and polished iOS and macOS apps. A web app is also available for cross-platform access.

Key Features

End-to-End Encryption

All notes are encrypted on your device before they reach Reflect's servers. Your encryption keys are derived from your credentials and never transmitted. Even Reflect employees cannot read your notes — a privacy guarantee backed by the technical architecture, not just policy.

Local AI Assistant (GPT-4)

The AI assistant is powered by GPT-4 but designed for privacy. When you ask Reflect AI to summarize a note or generate ideas, only the specific note content needed for that action is sent as a transient API call — not your entire knowledge base. There is no persistent AI training on your data.

Networked Notes & Backlinks

Notes reference each other using [[double bracket]] syntax, creating a web of bi-directional links. Every linked note automatically shows a backlinks panel listing all notes that reference it. The result is a personal knowledge graph you can navigate from any direction.

Daily Notes

A dated note is automatically created each day and becomes your working scratchpad for that day's thoughts, meetings, and tasks. Daily notes are linked in a chain, so you can navigate forward and backward in time. Calendar events are pulled in automatically, giving each day instant context.

Readwise & Calendar Sync

Reflect natively integrates with Readwise, automatically importing your book highlights, article annotations, and Kindle notes into your graph. Calendar sync (Google Calendar and Apple Calendar) pulls meeting titles and attendees directly into your daily note, making meeting note-taking frictionless.

iOS, Mac & Web Apps

Reflect ships polished native apps for iOS and macOS with offline support — your notes are always accessible even without internet. A web app covers Windows and Linux users. Sync is fast and conflict-free across all devices, with changes propagating in seconds.

Best Use Cases

Who Should Use Reflect?

  • Researchers and academics: Reflect's networked note structure excels at building literature reviews and connecting ideas across papers. The E2E encryption makes it appropriate for research that involves sensitive pre-publication findings, clinical data, or proprietary information.
  • Writers and journalists: The daily note system combined with bi-directional linking creates a natural research-to-draft pipeline. Writers can capture sources, quotes, and ideas in daily notes and link them into project-specific notes as pieces develop. The AI assistant helps expand rough ideas into fuller drafts.
  • Knowledge workers and consultants: Professionals who build deep expertise over time benefit enormously from Reflect's knowledge graph. Past meeting notes, client insights, and domain knowledge become searchable and linked — reducing the frustration of "I know I wrote this down somewhere."
  • Privacy-conscious professionals: Lawyers, therapists, healthcare workers, and executives who cannot afford to have their notes processed by a third-party AI or stored in plain text on a cloud server will find Reflect to be one of the only viable AI note-taking tools in the market. The E2E encryption is architecturally enforced, not just a policy promise.
  • Readers and lifelong learners: The Readwise integration makes Reflect the ideal home for your reading highlights. Import your Kindle highlights, Instapaper annotations, and podcast notes, then connect them to your own thoughts through the networked note system — building a genuine second brain from your reading practice.

Where Reflect May Not Be the Best Fit

  • Teams needing real-time collaborative editing should look at Notion or Confluence instead.
  • Users who need rich databases, kanban boards, or embedded spreadsheets will find Reflect's writing-first interface limiting.
  • Power users who rely on plugins and community extensions may prefer Obsidian's open plugin ecosystem.
  • Windows-primary users who want a native app rather than a web interface may be frustrated by the current Mac/iOS-only native clients.

Reflect vs. Obsidian

Obsidian stores notes as local Markdown files — giving you maximum control and portability — but its AI features depend on community plugins that vary in quality and may still send data to external servers. Reflect sacrifices the open-file approach for a polished, encrypted cloud sync that just works, with a first-party AI assistant that has been designed with privacy from the ground up. For non-technical users who want the benefits of networked notes without managing plugins or self-hosting, Reflect wins. For power users who want full file ownership and an infinite plugin ecosystem, Obsidian still leads.

Reflect vs. Notion AI

Notion is the productivity Swiss Army knife — databases, kanban, wikis, team collaboration — while Reflect is a focused writing and thinking tool. Notion AI processes your workspace data on Notion's servers with no encryption promise. For solo knowledge workers who prioritize writing and thinking over project management and team features, Reflect offers a better, more private experience at a lower price point.

Reflect vs. Roam Research

Roam pioneered the networked note-taking paradigm and still leads in graph visualization depth and the enthusiasm of its community. However, Roam has no encryption and limited AI integration. Reflect takes Roam's core concept — bi-directional linking and daily notes — and wraps it in a significantly more polished UI, solid native mobile apps, and a privacy-first AI layer. New users will find Reflect far more approachable; Roam loyalists tend to stay for the scripting and community.

Final Verdict

Our Recommendation

Reflect is one of the most thoughtfully designed note-taking applications in the privacy-first PKM space. It achieves something genuinely rare: combining the connected-thinking power of networked notes with architecturally enforced end-to-end encryption and an AI assistant that processes your data without storing it. For privacy-conscious professionals, researchers, and knowledge workers, Reflect is not just the best private AI note-taker — in many cases it is the only credible one.

The trade-offs are real: no collaboration, no free plan, no Windows native app, and a smaller ecosystem than Obsidian. But for solo knowledge builders who want their AI-assisted second brain to remain truly private, Reflect earns its $10/month comfortably. The polished iOS/Mac apps, automatic daily notes with calendar sync, and first-class Readwise integration make it a joy to use daily — not just a theoretically sound privacy choice.

Best for: Researchers, writers, lawyers, consultants, and any privacy-conscious knowledge worker who wants GPT-4-powered AI assistance on their notes without sending that knowledge to the cloud.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Reflect's end-to-end encryption mean the AI can't access my notes? +
Reflect's AI assistant uses a privacy-preserving approach: when you invoke the AI on a specific note, only the content of that note is sent as a transient API call to process your request. Reflect does not maintain a persistent copy of your notes in plaintext on its servers, and it does not train AI models on your data. The E2E encryption ensures that your notes at rest on Reflect's servers are encrypted with keys only you possess. The AI feature requires brief, temporary decryption on your device to prepare the API call — but this is architecturally very different from apps that store your notes in plain text for AI indexing.
Is there a free plan or free trial for Reflect? +
Reflect does not offer a permanent free plan — all features require a paid subscription. However, Reflect provides a 14-day free trial with full access to all features, including the AI assistant, encryption, and Readwise sync. After the trial, the single all-inclusive plan is $10/month (or $8.33/month billed annually at $100/year). There is no feature-limited free tier, which is the main accessibility drawback compared to tools like Obsidian (which has a free local version) or Notion (which has a free limited plan).
Does Reflect work on Windows and Android? +
Reflect has native apps for iOS and macOS, which are polished and support full offline access. For Windows and Android users, Reflect offers a progressive web app (PWA) accessible via browser. The web app is functional and sync-capable, but lacks some of the deeper OS integrations (Shortcuts, widgets, Share Sheet) available in the native apps. Android and Windows native apps are on the development roadmap but have not been released as of mid-2026. If you are primarily a Windows or Android user, this is a meaningful limitation to weigh before subscribing.
How does Reflect's bi-directional linking compare to Obsidian? +
Both Reflect and Obsidian use the [[double bracket]] convention for linking notes, and both create automatic backlinks panels. The key differences lie in the experience: Reflect's backlinks are native, fast, and displayed cleanly in the sidebar without requiring any plugin setup. Obsidian's core linking is also strong, but graph visualization, backlinks panels, and advanced linking features often depend on community plugins (some of which are excellent but require configuration). For users who want backlinks to just work immediately, Reflect is the more accessible choice. For users who want to customize every aspect of their graph with scripting and plugins, Obsidian has a deeper ceiling.
Can I import notes from Roam Research, Obsidian, or Notion into Reflect? +
Yes — Reflect supports importing from Roam Research (via JSON export), Obsidian (via Markdown files), and Notion (via exported Markdown or HTML). The import process preserves [[wikilinks]] and basic Markdown formatting. Complex Notion databases, Obsidian dataview tables, and Roam-specific syntax (like block references with ^caret IDs) may not import perfectly, but standard note content and links transfer well. Reflect's team has documented the import process for each source on their help site. For very large vaults, it is worth doing a test import with a subset of notes before migrating your full knowledge base.
