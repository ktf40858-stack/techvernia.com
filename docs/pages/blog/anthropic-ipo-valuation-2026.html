Anthropic Is Closing In on a $900 Billion Valuation — and a Historic IPO

From responsible AI lab to potential trillion-dollar company: how Claude's creator turned safety-first principles into the most compelling IPO story in tech.

5 min read
May 27, 2026
By Kodjo Apedoh

TL;DR: Anthropic is closing in on a $900 billion valuation and preparing for a potential IPO that could push that figure even higher. The company has reported its first quarterly operating profit, secured a $40 billion commitment from Google, and partnered with the Gates Foundation for $200 million in global impact initiatives. This is no longer just an AI safety story — it is the most consequential tech IPO in years.

For much of its short existence, Anthropic has positioned itself as the responsible alternative in the AI race — more safety-conscious than its competitors, more cautious in how it communicates capability gains, less interested in the hype cycle that surrounds every major model release. That identity remains intact. But the financial reality around the company in May 2026 is anything but modest.

Anthropic is currently closing in on a $900 billion valuation, according to multiple sources with knowledge of the company's ongoing investor discussions. That figure would place it among the most valuable private companies ever to exist in any industry. The company is also actively preparing for a potential initial public offering — one that could push that valuation even higher by the time shares reach public markets.

Anthropic has also reported its first quarterly operating profit, a milestone that meaningfully shifts the IPO narrative from sustainability questions to scale questions.

$200M Gates Foundation partnership over 4 years

The Numbers Behind the Moment

Several converging factors have driven Anthropic's trajectory. Google has committed up to $40 billion to the company — one of the largest single corporate investments in the history of the technology sector. In the same period, the Gates Foundation announced a $200 million, four-year partnership with Anthropic to develop AI tools for healthcare, education, and agriculture in underserved regions worldwide. That is not just capital. That is institutional credibility from one of the most recognized names in global philanthropy.

Claude, Anthropic's flagship model series, has driven rapid enterprise adoption across legal, financial, and research verticals. The company's API business is growing faster than internal projections, and its enterprise contracts — particularly in regulated sectors where safety and auditability are not optional — are proving far stickier than those of less cautious competitors.

Why Enterprise Is the Key

Safety as a Competitive Moat

In regulated industries — healthcare, legal, finance, government — AI auditability and predictable behavior are not optional features; they are procurement requirements. Anthropic's safety-first positioning, long seen as a constraint on growth, has turned into its most durable enterprise moat. Clients in these sectors are not switching providers: they are deepening their Claude integrations quarter after quarter.

What the Co-Founders Are Saying

The week before the valuation headlines broke, co-founder Jack Clark made a series of public predictions that have drawn considerable attention. Within twelve months, he believes an AI system will co-author a Nobel Prize-winning discovery. Within two years, bipedal robots will work alongside tradespeople on job sites. Within eighteen months, companies run entirely by AI will generate millions in annual revenue. And Clark places the probability above sixty percent that an AI model will fully train its own successor before the end of 2028.

These are not the words of a company hedging its bets. They are the words of a company that believes it is building something with civilization-scale implications — and that now has the financial foundation to pursue that ambition at full speed.

A Timeline That Would Have Seemed Impossible Two Years Ago

Co-founder Jack Clark's public predictions in May 2026: an AI co-authoring a Nobel Prize discovery within 12 months; bipedal robots working on job sites within 24 months; fully AI-run companies generating millions in annual revenue within 18 months; and a greater than 60% probability that an AI model fully trains its own successor by end of 2028. These are extraordinary claims — made by someone with full visibility into what Anthropic's models can already do.

The IPO That Changes Everything

The potential Anthropic IPO is not just a financial event. It is a referendum on the long-term viability of the safety-first approach to AI development. For years, critics argued that Anthropic's caution would cost it market share and revenue. The data now says otherwise. The company's first quarterly operating profit, its explosive API growth, and the stickiness of its enterprise contracts all tell a consistent story: safety and commercial success are not in tension. They are aligned.

If Anthropic reaches public markets at or near its current private valuation, it will also send a clear message to every AI startup that followed OpenAI's growth-at-all-costs model: there is more than one path to the frontier, and responsibility is not a handicap.

TechVernia Verdict

Anthropic's march toward a $900 billion valuation and IPO is the most consequential corporate story in AI since the founding of the company itself. The first quarterly profit, the $40 billion Google commitment, and the Gates Foundation partnership combine to paint a picture of a company that has navigated the most competitive technology landscape in history without compromising its founding principles — and won.

The IPO, when it comes, will be worth watching not just as a financial milestone, but as a signal about what the AI industry will reward in the decade ahead. The early data suggests the market is ready to bet big on responsibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Anthropic's current valuation?

Has Anthropic reported a profit?

Yes. Anthropic has reported its first quarterly operating profit — a significant milestone that transforms the IPO narrative. The profitability is driven by rapid enterprise adoption of Claude across legal, financial, and research sectors, as well as strong growth in the API business. This is particularly notable given the enormous compute costs involved in training and running frontier AI models.

Why did Google invest $40 billion in Anthropic?

Google's $40 billion commitment to Anthropic represents one of the largest single corporate investments in the history of the technology sector. The strategic rationale is multifaceted: Anthropic's Claude models provide a competitive alternative to OpenAI's products for Google Cloud customers; Anthropic's safety research aligns with Google's long-term concerns about AI governance; and the investment gives Google significant influence over the direction of one of the two most credible frontier AI labs outside its own DeepMind division.

What did the Gates Foundation partnership involve?

The Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation announced a $200 million, four-year partnership with Anthropic to develop AI tools specifically for healthcare, education, and agriculture in underserved regions worldwide. This is not a philanthropic donation — it is a structured partnership aimed at deploying Claude-based systems in contexts where AI could have the highest humanitarian impact. The partnership also provides Anthropic with significant institutional credibility beyond the technology sector.

What are Jack Clark's predictions for the next two years?

Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark made several striking predictions in May 2026: that an AI system will co-author a Nobel Prize-winning scientific discovery within twelve months; that companies run entirely by AI will generate millions in annual revenue within eighteen months; that bipedal robots will work alongside human tradespeople on job sites within two years; and that there is a greater than 60% probability an AI model will fully train its own successor by the end of 2028. These predictions, made by someone with direct visibility into Anthropic's capabilities, suggest the company sees its current trajectory as leading to far more transformative outcomes than its current valuation implies.
