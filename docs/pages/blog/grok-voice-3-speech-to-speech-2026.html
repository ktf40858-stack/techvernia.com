Grok Voice 3.0: The AI Voice Agent That Wants to Answer Your Phone

On October 14, SpaceXSI, the company formerly known as xAI, released Grok Voice 3.0, a real-time speech-to-speech model for voice agents. It tops the Artificial Analysis Speech-to-Speech Index at the same $0.08 per minute as its predecessor, but OpenAI still wins the test closest to real support calls. Here is what it does, what the numbers really say, and how to test it before you trust it with your customers.

The Next AI Interface Is a Phone Call

Most people still meet AI through a chat box. But a huge share of real business still happens by voice: support lines, sales calls, appointment booking, order tracking. That is where the AI labs are now fighting, and the weapon of choice is the speech-to-speech model.

On October 14, 2026, SpaceXSI released Grok Voice 3.0, the new model behind its Voice Agent API, with the model ID grok-voice-3.0. The company calls it "the world's most intelligent speech-to-speech model." It listens and answers in real time, without converting your voice to text first and back to speech after.

A quick word on the name. SpaceX acquired xAI in February 2026. According to SiliconANGLE, the AI unit was renamed SpaceXAI in July and took the SpaceXSI name in early October. Grok is the same product family; only the company label changed. Many outlets still say "xAI."

What Grok Voice 3.0 Is

Grok Voice 3.0 is not a voice assistant app. It is a model that developers plug into their own products through an API, to build voice agents: software that picks up a call, understands what the caller wants, and handles it.

  • Real-time conversation. It handles pauses, turn-taking and interruptions, the moments where older voice bots talk over you or freeze.
  • Built for agents, not demos. The company pitches it for customer support, sales calls and in-app assistants.
  • Tools during the call. According to the Voice Agent API documentation, sessions support audio and text, custom voices, function calls, search and MCP tools. In plain terms, the agent can look things up and take actions while it talks.
  • Same price as before. $0.08 per minute, unchanged from Grok Voice 2.0.
  • Automatic upgrade. The alias grok-voice-latest now points to 3.0, and Grok Voice 2.0 is deprecated. Agents on the alias were moved over with no code changes.

Why It Matters

From Phone Menu to Phone Agent

"Press 1 for billing" is a decision tree. A voice agent with tools is something else: it can verify who you are, check your order, issue a credit and book a technician in one natural conversation. Grok Voice 3.0 is one more sign that this jump is no longer experimental. The big labs now ship it as a product with a price per minute.

Grok leads on most lines, and the jump from version 2.0 is real: its τ-Voice score went from 56.5% to 71.6%. ServiceNow also reports that it reached 74% accuracy on EVA-Bench, its open benchmark of more than 200 enterprise scenarios, the highest result so far.

But look at the last line. τ-Voice simulates real customer-support calls, with accents and background noise. That is the test closest to what most companies will actually deploy, and OpenAI's GPT-Live-1 still wins it. Sounding natural and solving the caller's problem are two different skills.

What to read with care. The benchmark table comes from the company's announcement, citing Artificial Analysis. The sub-second latency, the 40% speed advantage over GPT-Live-1 on τ-Voice, and all deployment figures below are company-reported and have not been independently verified. Leaderboards in voice AI move every few weeks.

Where It Already Runs

Being part of the SpaceX group gives Grok Voice something most rivals do not have: large in-house customers.

  • Tesla: the company says Grok Voice handles millions of conversations per day across Tesla and SpaceX, including in-car requests like checking a calendar or replying to email.
  • Grok Bot: its voice mode now runs on Grok Voice 3.0.
  • Starlink: a single voice agent reportedly handles about 20,000 customer calls per day in several languages. It verifies customers, troubleshoots hardware, issues credits and ships replacement equipment. The company says about 70% of calls are resolved without a human and about one in four sales calls ends in a purchase.

If those Starlink numbers hold up, they are the most interesting part of the launch. They describe a voice agent doing a full support job end to end, not answering trivia.

Cost: What $0.08 per Minute Means

At $0.08 per minute, a 5-minute support call costs about $0.40 in model fees. 10,000 such calls per month comes to about $4,000. Artificial Analysis also measured cost per task on Big Bench Audio: about $0.025 for Grok Voice 3.0, against $0.030 for GPT-Live-1 and $0.055 for OpenAI's GPT-Realtime-2.1.

Remember that the model is only one line of the bill. Telephony, your own backend, monitoring, and the humans who take over escalated calls still cost money. The right metric is not price per minute but cost per resolved call.

Use Cases

Where a Voice Agent Earns Its Place

  • Support teams: simple, repetitive calls (order status, password resets, delivery questions) that eat human minutes.
  • Startups: booking, ordering or onboarding by phone without hiring a call center.
  • Small businesses: restaurants, clinics and garages that miss calls after hours.
  • Developers using MCP: teams that already expose tools to text agents and now want to add a voice front end.
  • Product teams: in-app voice assistants that need to feel natural, not robotic.

How I Would Test It

1. Use Real Scripts

Take ten real calls from your support queue: the easy ones, the angry ones and the confusing ones. Do not test with the vendor's demo script.

2. Try to Break It

Interrupt it mid-sentence. Change your mind halfway. Speak with an accent, with background noise, on a bad connection. This is exactly what τ-Voice measures, and where models still differ.

3. Compare Side by Side

Run the same calls through Grok Voice 3.0 and at least one alternative, such as GPT-Live-1 or Gemini Live. Measure three things: was the problem solved, how many times did it lose the thread, and what did each resolved call cost.

4. Pin the Version and Keep a Human Exit

The grok-voice-latest alias switched models automatically on October 14. For production, pin grok-voice-3.0 so behavior does not change under your feet. And always give callers a clear path to a human, especially when the agent can issue refunds or change accounts.

Grok Voice 3.0 raises the bar for voice agents at no extra cost. It leads most benchmarks and adds tools during the call, but OpenAI still wins on realistic support calls. Test it on your own calls before you hand it your customers.

My Take

As a network engineer, I spend a lot of time thinking about latency. In voice, latency is everything. A one-second pause feels natural; a three-second pause feels like a broken line, and the caller starts talking over the machine. That is why benchmarks like Full Duplex Bench matter more to me than raw intelligence scores.

What makes Grok Voice 3.0 interesting is not a single number. It is the combination: better quality at the same price, plus actions during the call, plus a giant in-house testing ground in Tesla and Starlink. Voice is becoming the next battleground of AI agents, and OpenAI, Google and SpaceXSI are now only a few points apart.

My advice: do not replace your call center on Monday. Run a small pilot on your real calls, measure cost per resolved call, keep a human exit, and compare at least two providers. The winner in this market will change every few months, so avoid locking yourself in.
