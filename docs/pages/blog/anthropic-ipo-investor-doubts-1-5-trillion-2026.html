Investors Doubt Anthropic Can List in November. The Filing Shows Why

Reuters reports that potential investors doubt Anthropic can complete its roadshow in time for a November listing. Its amended S-1 shows a company growing at remarkable speed, while carrying about $895 billion in compute commitments. Here is what the numbers say, what they do not say, and what to watch next.

The Bell Has Not Rung Yet

Anthropic's roadshow was expected to begin the week of November 9, with pricing the following week. On November 6, Reuters reported that potential investors doubt that schedule can still produce a November listing, citing four people familiar with the discussions.

If the AI market stays under pressure, Reuters says the offering could wait until conditions look steadier, possibly past year-end. These are investor doubts, not a company announcement. But they arrive at a moment when both the price and the market around it are changing.

From $2 Trillion to a Question Mark

Earlier this year, investors expected an October listing at a valuation as high as $2 trillion. The figure recently under discussion is $1.5 trillion, and Reuters says some investors now see that as the high end.

The offering could reach $100 billion, according to Reuters, but Business Insider notes that the amended filing does not yet include a price range, share count or offering size. So any valuation you read today comes from market discussions, not from a price Anthropic has set.

A quick calculation shows what is at stake. Multiply Q3 revenue by four and you get about $68 billion. At $1.5 trillion, that would be roughly 22 times annualized revenue. This is my own back-of-the-envelope figure, not a number from the filing, and it ignores growth. But it shows how much future success that price assumes.

What the Amended S-1 Shows

Anthropic filed the amendment on November 2. The growth is real: Q3 revenue reached $16.96 billion, up 47% from $11.56 billion in Q2 and more than 12 times the $1.38 billion of a year earlier.

Metric, three months to Sept. 30What the filing shows
Revenue$16.96 billion, up from $11.56 billion in Q2.
Operating resultA $567 million loss, compared with a $974 million profit in Q2.
Adjusted operating income$2.46 billion, a 15% margin, on Anthropic's own adjusted measure. It excludes stock compensation, non-cash charitable contributions and the Bartz settlement.
Net loss$23.29 billion in Q3. Over nine months, the $104.04 billion net loss was mostly driven by a $94.32 billion non-cash fair-value expense.
Compute and infrastructure$9.86 billion, or 58% of revenue, up from 48% in Q2.

Read the net loss carefully. The nine-month figure is dominated by non-cash fair-value changes on convertible notes, derivative liabilities and SAFEs as the valuation rose. It looks enormous, but it says little about whether selling AI is profitable. The compute line tells you more.

Key Point

The Real Number Is the Commitment

Anthropic lists about $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and leases, substantially all for compute and infrastructure. About $69.8 billion is due within 12 months of September 30, about $447.5 billion before the end of 2030, and most of the rest runs from 2031 through 2045. By my calculation, the total is roughly 13 times annualized Q3 revenue.

The filing also describes the mismatch: revenue is primarily consumption-based and much of it carries no long-term contractual commitment, while compute commitments generally extend over multiple years. Customers can reduce usage. Those infrastructure obligations are harder to shrink.

That does not mean the commitments are reckless. Reserving capacity is how a frontier lab makes sure it can serve demand, and Anthropic says that buying $5 billion of short-term compute in Q3 cost significantly more than comparable multiyear capacity. Too little compute is expensive as well. The risk runs in both directions, which is why investors want to see the next revenue number.

The Line Everyone Is Waiting For

Reuters says investors are waiting for Anthropic's October revenue, which was left blank in the updated S-1, to judge whether growth has recovered after a recent slowdown. The prospectus also warns that revenue growth may slow and that the company will continue to incur losses.

The filing includes two other details worth watching. According to MLQ.ai, an estimated 5% to 7% of nine-month revenue came from customers later found to be violating usage policies or commercial terms, so stricter enforcement could reduce reported revenue. And cloud marketplaces accounted for 44% of revenue, which means a disruption there could affect collections and revenue.

A Harder Market

This is not only about Anthropic. AI-related stocks fell for two days after the US midterm elections, and more than 50 technology stocks in the S&P 1500 dropped 10% or more, according to Reuters.

  • Tech versus the market: tech has underperformed the S&P 500 by 10.7% over the past month, the most extreme one-month gap since May 2002.
  • The mood: Keith Lerner of Truist Advisory Services told Reuters, "We are now seeing something of a buyers' strike in tech."
  • Retail investors: Vanda Research recorded the strongest retail selling since the Covid-19 pandemic.
  • Credit markets: credit default swaps covering SpaceX and CoreWeave hit records.
  • Politics: AP reports that restrictions passed in 44 of the 45 local data center referendums where counting was complete, and that the Nasdaq stood 11% below its all-time high on Thursday.
The other side. A later listing is not a failed listing. Some analysts expect strong retail demand, as SpaceX saw, and Rick Meckler of Cherry Lane Investments told Reuters: "They're going to become public." The open questions are when, and at what price.

What to Watch Next

  1. The roadshow: does it begin this week, or does the date move again?
  2. The October revenue figure: did growth recover after the slowdown investors are concerned about?
  3. The price range: the first public range will show how far expectations have really moved from $1.5 trillion.
  4. The discount: Reuters says Anthropic may need to offer a bigger one to win commitments.
  5. The wider AI trade: Reuters notes that a delay could heighten concern about the AI ecosystem and weigh on large tech-heavy stocks.

My Take

For two years, the AI market mostly asked one question: how powerful will the models get? An S-1 asks different ones. How much does each dollar of revenue cost? Who has committed to pay next year? What do you owe in 2030, whatever happens to demand?

As a network engineer, I read that $895 billion less as a finance number and more as an infrastructure bet. Capacity is planned years ahead, while traffic can change in a quarter. Every engineer knows that tension: build too little and you fail your users, build too much and you pay for idle equipment. Anthropic is managing that same trade-off, at a scale few companies have ever attempted.

None of this tells us the IPO will fail or that the price is wrong. A company that grows revenue 12 times in a year has earned serious attention. But for the first time, the AI boom is being checked line by line, in public. And right now, the most interesting line is the blank one.

Would you buy at $1.5 trillion, or wait for the October number?
