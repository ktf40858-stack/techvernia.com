SASE & AI in 2026: How Fortinet, Palo Alto, and Cisco Are Rebuilding Network Security

AI-powered SASE is no longer a roadmap promise. Three vendors are doing it differently — and the gaps are now measurable.

5 min read
May 8, 2026
By Kodjo Apedoh

Three years ago, SASE was a Gartner acronym vendors raced to stamp on their products. The concept — converging SD-WAN with Security Service Edge into a cloud-delivered architecture — promised to eliminate legacy backhaul, replacing it with zero trust access, secure web gateways, and firewall-as-a-service at the edge. The gap between that promise and real deployments came down to one thing: real-time decisions at scale without latency. In 2026, AI is closing that gap.

Fortinet, Palo Alto Networks, and Cisco have each embedded AI into their SASE stacks at the infrastructure level — not as a feature layer, but as the engine behind threat detection, policy enforcement, and traffic optimization. The results are now diverging in ways that matter for network engineers making platform decisions.

Fortinet: Depth Through Telemetry

Vendor 1

FortiAI & FortiSASE

FortiAI is embedded directly into FortiOS and surfaces through FortiSASE. Fortinet's advantage is data scale: over a decade of global telemetry feeds detection models processing more than 100 billion events per day, delivering faster zero-day identification and cross-environment threat correlation. FortiSASE architecture is now a core topic in the 2026 NSE7 exam track.

Palo Alto Networks: Behavioral Intelligence

Vendor 2

Prisma SASE & ADEM

Prisma SASE leads with behavioral AI. Models trained on user, application, and device patterns flag deviations that signature-based systems miss. The ADEM layer — Autonomous Digital Experience Management — correlates performance degradation with security events, a practical differentiator in hybrid environments where a misconfiguration and an active threat can look identical. Prisma SASE policy management is central to the 2026 PCNSE track.

Cisco: Integration as the Core Argument

Vendor 3

Cisco+ Secure Connect

Cisco+ Secure Connect ties SASE into SecureX and ThousandEyes. The argument: a security platform with incomplete network visibility cannot make reliable decisions about any part of that network. AI powers automated policy recommendations, encrypted traffic analysis without decryption, and cross-platform threat correlation. For CCNP Security and CCIE candidates, Cisco+ Secure Connect architecture now appears in lab scenarios.

What This Means for Network Engineers

Platform choice is rarely purely technical. But the AI layer is now a real differentiator — specifically in how much manual tuning each platform requires to stay effective. Platforms with deeper AI integration into policy automation demand less ongoing intervention, and that compounds across distributed environments. For certification candidates, AI-driven threat detection within SASE is core curriculum across NSE7, PCNSE, and CCNP Security.

The Bottom Line

SASE was a concept. AI has made it operational. The question for network teams in 2026 is not whether to adopt an AI-powered SASE platform — it is which platform's approach best fits the environment they are protecting.
